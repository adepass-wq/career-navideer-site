FEATURED PERSPECTIVE

Track decisions, not just clicks.

By Anthony DePass, Ph.D.

Most career platforms can tell us that a learner logged in, completed a quiz or viewed an occupation. Those metrics matter, but they do not reveal how a decision develops.

Career decision-making is iterative. A learner may express a desired lifestyle, explore widely, return to compare options, narrow a direction and investigate the education needed to act. That sequence tells a richer story than a single score.

Career Decision Intelligence makes those observable patterns useful while respecting a critical boundary: behavior can inform support, but it should not be used to diagnose a learner or claim certainty about their future.

CAREER EXPLORATION

Why lifestyle belongs in the career conversation

A career is not chosen in isolation. Housing, location, family goals, transportation, financial expectations and work values all shape whether a pathway is sustainable. Starting with life makes tradeoffs visible before a title becomes a commitment.

The takeaway: Better career planning connects aspiration to economic and educational reality without shrinking the learner’s sense of possibility.

WORKFORCE CHANGE

Career navigation must become more adaptive

Technology, skills-based hiring and new credential pathways are changing how people enter and move through work. Career tools must support trades, certificates, college, reskilling and advanced study - not assume one degree-driven route.

The takeaway: Navigation works best when existing skills, desired work, labor-market information and preparation options can be considered together.

IDENTITY & OWNERSHIP

Exploration deepens when learners can make it their own

Personal choices - how a learner imagines life, shapes a profile and returns to reconsider options - can anchor deeper exploration. For younger learners this often supports discovery; for adults it may signal a highly practical transition.

The takeaway: Ownership does not guarantee an outcome. It creates a more meaningful environment in which a learner can explore, compare and act.
