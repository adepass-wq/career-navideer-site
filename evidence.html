Evidence & Career Decision Intelligence

Study the process, not just the final choice.

Career Navideer creates a trail of aspirations, exploration and decisions. Career Decision Intelligence (CDI) connects those observable actions to the learner's context, stage and next decision - without reducing a person to a score.

Career Decision Intelligence (CDI) in one view

From interaction data to decision intelligence.

CDI means Career Decision Intelligence: a framework for understanding how career decisions develop through observable actions over time. A click shows that a person visited; CDI examines what the learner expressed, explored, compared, reconsidered and investigated next. It is decision evidence, not a psychological diagnosis.

One platform, different developmental work

Career exploration changes with the question a person is trying to answer.

The same Career Navideer tools serve different purposes across life stages. CDI keeps those differences visible when interpreting behavior.

A journey, not a one-time result

Career exploration can deepen, narrow and change direction.

The emerging evidence model treats career exploration as iterative. Learners may revisit earlier questions as their identity, circumstances and understanding of available pathways change.

01Identity and foundation

Express interests, lifestyle priorities and the future a learner wants to consider.

02Exploration and growth

Discover occupations broadly, return to possibilities and build a frame of reference.

03Refinement and comparison

Compare options, reconsider assumptions and narrow choices without forcing certainty.

04Pathways and action

Investigate education, credentials and practical next steps connected to a direction.

These stages are conditional milestones, not a required sequence or a score. Movement can be forward, backward or cyclical.

Responsible interpretation

Behavioral evidence, with clear boundaries.

What we can observe

  • Identity and lifestyle interactions
  • Occupation breadth and comparison
  • Return sessions and iterative cycles
  • Credential and education investigation
  • Changes in choices over time

What we do not infer

  • Emotion or internal mental state
  • Personality labels
  • Psychological diagnosis
  • Guaranteed readiness or outcomes
  • Causation from descriptive patterns
