Evidence & impact

From career choice to career decision intelligence.

Career Decision Intelligence (CDI) is a framework for organizing what can be learned about how people develop career direction over time. It is broader than a one-time match and intentionally separates what is observed from what is inferred.

Important: CDI is not presented here as a validated score, diagnosis, fit measure, prediction system or causal-effectiveness claim. Current support is strongest conceptually, operationally and descriptively; longitudinal outcome evidence remains preliminary.
Six domains

A fuller view of career development.

1. Aspirational Intelligence

What learners want from adult life, work and economic independence.

2. Exploration Intelligence

What possibilities learners investigate, compare, revisit or ignore.

3. Developmental Intelligence

How confidence, readiness and career-development capacities change over time when linked measures are available.

4. Decision Intelligence

What choices learners make, revise or stabilize during exploration.

5. Alignment Intelligence

How lifestyle, career, education and pathway choices fit—or fail to fit—one another.

6. Outcome Intelligence

What later educational, persistence, completion or career outcomes can eventually be connected to the earlier process.

What Career Navideer natively observes

Evaluation model

Designed to support stronger questions.

Inputs

Student context, aspirations, program setting and baseline measures.

Activities

Lifestyle design, career exploration, pathway review and repeated decision points.

Outputs

Exploration breadth, selected pathways, revisions, usage patterns and documented choices.

Developmental outcomes

Where appropriate, link external measures such as career decision-making self-efficacy, pathway clarity or adaptability.

Longer-term outcomes

Persistence, completion, education choices and career-related outcomes as longitudinal evidence matures.
